Few forces in Mantic's Halo: Flashpoint skirmish game hit as hard or as fast as the Jiralhanae, and players who love close-quarters aggression keep coming back to them. Halo Flashpoint Brutes are the faction for anyone who wants to close the distance, break the enemy line, and win the fight before their opponent can settle into a plan. This guide covers where Brutes fit in the game, what roles they fill on the table, and how to build a warband that wins scenarios instead of just looking terrifying.

## What Makes Halo Flashpoint Brutes Different?

In Halo lore, the Jiralhanae are towering, pack-oriented warriors from the world of Doisac. They served the Covenant as shock troops and enforcers, then became the backbone of the Banished, the mercenary coalition led by Atriox. That history shapes how they feel on the tabletop: blunt, relentless, and built to take ground rather than hold it politely.

Halo: Flashpoint is a skirmish game, which means every model matters. There is no disposable tide of infantry to hide behind, and a Brute caught in the open is often a Brute you do not get back. Their identity is momentum. You trade carefully for a turn or two, then commit hard and let the resulting board control snowball.

Community reports from Flashpoint players tend to describe the same experience: Brutes feel forgiving early and punishing late. If you build a lead, they are excellent at keeping it. If you fall behind, they struggle to claw back through ranged play alone.

| What Brutes Do Well | Where Brutes Struggle |
|---|---|
| Winning melee exchanges through raw close-combat pressure | Trading shots at long range against dedicated shooters |
| Absorbing damage and staying in the fight | Protecting fragile support models once the line breaks |
| Punishing opponents who clump up or leave gaps | Playing a slow, reactive, hold-back game |
| Snowballing once they gain board control | Recovering after a badly ordered activation sequence |

## The Core Brute Archetypes on the Table

Most Halo Flashpoint Brutes forces are built around a handful of recognizable archetypes. Exact profiles, weapon options, and points live on the unit cards that come in your box — always read those before finalizing a list — but the battlefield roles themselves stay consistent from game to game.

| Archetype | Primary Role | Best Used For | Watch Out For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Line Warrior | Objective holder and melee body | Contesting central objectives and soaking early fire | Getting isolated; they want a second model nearby |
| Captain / Chieftain | Leader and force multiplier | Ordering activations, supporting nearby warriors, taking duels | Overextending — losing your leader early is usually fatal |
| Heavy-Weapon Brute | Ranged deterrent | Suppressing lanes and softening targets before the charge | Slow repositioning; easy to out-maneuver |
| Jump-Pack Brute | Mobile threat and flanker | Leaping onto objectives and hunting backline models | Fragile in a straight fight after the jump |
| Elite Bodyguard | Line-breaker | Crashing into your opponent's best unit | High investment — every loss stings |

The temptation with Brutes is to take one of everything. Resist it. A focused list that does two things well beats a scattered list that does five things badly, especially in a game where a single wasted activation can decide a scenario.

## Brutes vs. Elites: Choosing Your Aggression

Brutes and Elites are the two great close-combat identities in Halo: Flashpoint, and they are frequently compared because both can appear in Banished-aligned forces. They are not interchangeable. Elites reward precision and positioning; Brutes reward pressure and tempo.

| Trait | Halo Flashpoint Brutes | Elites (Sangheili) |
|---|---|---|
| Fantasy | Pack brawlers who win by force | Disciplined duelists who win by finesse |
| Preferred engagement range | Close, immediately | Close to mid, on their terms |
| Resilience style | Tough and forgiving of small errors | Rewards correct positioning more than raw toughness |
| Mobility | Moderate, spiked by jump-pack units | High and consistent |
| Skill floor | Low — easy to pick up and play | Medium — timing and matchups matter |
| Best for | Players who like pressure and tempo | Players who like precision and counterplay |

Because both can sit in the same broader faction, the real question is not "which is better" but "which one anchors my plan." If you want a durable core that walks forward and dares your opponent to stop it, Brutes are the answer. If you want a scalpel, look at Elites and use Brutes as the hammer that follows up.

## How to Build a Halo Flashpoint Brutes Warband

Flashpoint warbands are small, so every slot is precious. Build in priority order and resist the urge to spend your last points on a shiny upgrade instead of a body that can stand on an objective.

| Priority | Why It Matters | Practical Approach |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A dependable leader | Losing your leader early usually ends your scenario | Take a Captain or Chieftain and keep them within support range of the line |
| 2. Two objective-capable units | Skirmish games are won on objectives, not kill counts | Line Warriors that can sit on a point and survive a full round |
| 3. One ranged threat | Pure melee lists get shot apart crossing open ground | A heavy-weapon Brute that makes your opponent think twice about standing still |
| 4. One mobile piece | You need an answer to a fast opponent | A jump-pack unit or a cheap skirmisher to contest the far side |
| 5. Activation count | More activations usually means more board control | Trim upgrades before you trim models |

A few general principles that hold up across most scenarios:

- **Assign every model a job before the first activation.** "Fight stuff" is not a job. "Hold the left objective for two rounds" is.
- **Keep your leader alive longer than feels necessary.** Their value compounds over the course of a game.
- **Do not buy a melee upgrade for a model you intend to keep in cover.** Points spent on unused abilities are points your opponent gets for free.
- **Check the scenario deck before you finalize your list.** Objective-heavy missions reward bodies; assassination-style missions reward a concentrated hammer.

If you are brand new to the game, start with the models in the core set and add Brute units once you understand how activations flow. Mantic Games' official site is the best place to confirm what is currently in production and in stock.

## Tactics, Timing, and Common Mistakes

The single biggest difference between a Brute player who wins and one who loses is patience. Brutes look like a turn-one charge faction. They are not. They are a turn-two-and-three faction that happens to hit very hard.

### The Three-Phase Turn Rhythm

1. **Secure.** Use the first round to move into cover, claim objectives, and force your opponent to commit first.
2. **Trade.** Take favorable melee exchanges where you can bring two models to bear on one. Never trade one-for-one if you can avoid it.
3. **Commit.** Once you have a numerical edge on one flank, push everything through that gap. Brutes win by concentrating force, not by spreading it.

| Common Mistake | Why It Costs You the Game | The Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Charging on turn one | You arrive piecemeal and get focused down one model at a time | Advance behind cover, then commit two units together |
| Ignoring the scenario | Kills do not score points on their own | Give each unit a scenario task before you roll any dice |
| Clumping models together | A single area-effect attack or a bad melee pile-up wrecks your line | Leave gaps between models and approach from two angles |
| Investing everything in one star model | Losing it removes your entire plan | Spread your threat across two or three units |
| Forgetting leader abilities | You are leaving free efficiency unused every round | Read your leader's card at the start of each round |

Player experience suggests that Brute lists also benefit enormously from terrain. If you are setting up the board yourself, include enough line-of-sight blocking pieces to cross the middle without being shot to pieces. On a wide-open table, Brutes are simply worse — that is not a faction flaw, it is a table setup problem.

## Painting and Collecting the Jiralhanae

Brutes are one of the most forgiving factions to paint, which makes them a great entry point for newcomers. Their armor is bulky and textured, so a base coat, a wash, and a drybrush already produce a table-ready result.

A simple, effective recipe for most Brute armor:

- Prime in a mid-gray or dark brown depending on whether you want Banished or Covenant-era coloring.
- Base coat the armor plates, then apply a dark wash to pick out the recesses.
- Drybrush a lighter tone across the top edges to create contrast.
- Pick out skin, teeth, and weapon glow with one or two bright accents so the models read clearly at arm's length.

Do not skip the bases. A consistent base scheme ties a warband together and makes your Halo Flashpoint Brutes look like a cohesive army rather than a random collection of models.

## FAQ

**Are Halo Flashpoint Brutes good for beginners?**

Yes. Their straightforward game plan — move forward, hold objectives, win melee — means new players do not need to master complex synergy chains to have a competitive game. The main skill to learn is patience with your charge timing.

**Can I mix Brutes with Elites and Grunts in the same list?**

Generally yes, since Brutes typically appear in Banished-aligned forces that also include Sangheili and Unggoy units. Mixing gives you ranged support and mobility that a pure Brute list lacks. Check your faction rules and unit cards for any restrictions before you commit.

**How many Brute models should I bring?**

That depends entirely on the points limit and the scenario. As a rule, secure your leader and two objective-capable units first, then add specialists. More activations is almost always better than more upgrades.

**How do I beat a Brute-heavy list?**

Deny them the charge. Use long sightlines, chokepoints, and focused fire to remove one model at a time before it reaches you. Halo Flashpoint Brutes are at their weakest when they have to cross open ground under fire, so make them do exactly that.
