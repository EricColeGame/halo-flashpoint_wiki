Few things in tabletop gaming feel better than marching a line of Elites up the board while a screen of Grunts eats the incoming fire. The **Halo Flashpoint Covenant** faction delivers exactly that fantasy: a mixed-species force where every model has a defined job and where positioning matters more than raw stats. If you are building your first alien list or trying to finally crack that Spartan player in your local group, this guide covers the units, the archetypes, and the tactics that make the **Halo Flashpoint Covenant** work.

Halo Flashpoint is Mantic Games' skirmish miniatures game set in the Halo universe, launched in 2024 after a crowdfunding campaign and supported since with a growing range of expansions. You can browse the current releases on [Mantic Games' official site](https://www.manticgames.com/). The Covenant side of the range is built around the faction's iconic species — Sangheili, Unggoy, and Kig-Yar, with heavier Jiralhanae and Mgalekgolo options rounding things out. If you want the lore behind those names, [Halo Waypoint](https://www.halowaypoint.com/) is the authoritative source.

## What Makes the Halo Flashpoint Covenant Faction Unique

Most skirmish games sell you symmetry: two forces with the same rules and different paint jobs. Halo Flashpoint does not do that. Spartans are a small, durable, elite handful. The Covenant are more numerous, more fragile individually, and far more specialized. Community reports consistently describe the faction as a "toolbox" army — you win by having the right answer in the right place, not by having the best single model.

| Aspect | Spartan Fireteams | Halo Flashpoint Covenant |
|---|---|---|
| Model count | Low, elite | Higher, mixed quality |
| Durability | High per model | Varies wildly by species |
| Ranged output | Consistent, accurate | Spiky, weapon-dependent |
| Melee threat | Strong but limited | Broad, with dedicated killers |
| Learning curve | Forgiving | Punishing but rewarding |
| Best at | Focused aggression | Attrition, objectives, board control |

That asymmetry is the whole point. A Covenant player who tries to trade shots evenly with Spartans usually loses. A Covenant player who baits, swarms, and trades cheap models for expensive ones usually wins.

## Covenant Unit Roles, Explained

### Sangheili (Elites)

Elites are the backbone of almost every Halo Flashpoint Covenant list. They combine respectable durability with genuine melee threat, and they are the models most likely to hold a contested objective under pressure. Player experience suggests Elites are the safest place to put your leader and your most aggressive plays — they can absorb a bad round and still contribute. Plasma rifles and energy swords give them flexibility at both range bands.

### Unggoy (Grunts)

Grunts are cheap, expendable, and quietly essential. Their job is not to kill things; it is to stand on objectives, block charge lanes, and force your opponent to spend activations removing models that were never going to win a fight anyway. A Grunt that dies protecting an Elite has done its job perfectly.

### Kig-Yar (Jackals)

Jackals are the faction's scalpel. Shield gauntlets make them annoyingly hard to shift from cover, while marksman-style loadouts let them punish exposed targets at range. They are the unit that punishes opponents for leaving a wounded Spartan in the open.

### Jiralhanae and Mgalekgolo (Brutes and Hunters)

When you need something to walk into the middle of the board and dare your opponent to deal with it, this is where you look. Brutes bring aggression and melee pressure; Hunters bring the heaviest armor in the faction. Both are expensive enough that you feel every loss, so they should be committed with a plan rather than thrown forward on impulse.

| Unit | Battlefield Job | Key Strength | Watch Out For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elites | Anchor, leader, duelist | Balanced stats, melee threat | Getting isolated |
| Grunts | Objective holder, screen | Low cost, numbers | Breaking under pressure |
| Jackals | Ranged harassment | Cover + shields | Melee engagements |
| Brutes | Shock assault | Aggression, close-range damage | Overextending |
| Hunters | Line breaker, tank | Heavy armor | Being ignored and out-maneuvered |

## Building a Halo Flashpoint Covenant Force

There is no single correct list. What matters is that your force has answers to three questions: Who holds objectives? Who kills things? Who absorbs the first punch? If a list cannot answer all three, it will struggle.

| Archetype | Core Units | Playstyle | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Swarm Control | Grunts + Jackals | Flood objectives, trade cheap models | Objective-heavy scenarios |
| Elite Spearhead | Elites + Brutes | Aggressive, mid-board pressure | Players who like tempo |
| Hammer and Anvil | Hunters + Grunts | Tanky anchor plus disposable screen | Grinding, defensive play |
| Balanced Toolbox | Mixed Elites, Jackals, Grunts | Flexible, reactive | New players learning the faction |

A practical way to start is to fill five slots and adjust from there:

| Slot | Option A | Option B | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leader | Elite | Brute | Keeps your force coherent |
| Anchor | Hunter | Elite | Soaks the first exchange |
| Ranged | Jackal marksman | Elite with rifle | Punishes exposed models |
| Screen | Grunt squad | Grunt squad | Protects your investment |
| Flex | Brute | Second Jackal | Adapts to your opponent |

A few force-building habits separate winning Covenant lists from losing ones:

- **Never take more Grunts than you can protect.** They are screens, not an army.
- **Decide your leader before you buy models.** Leaders shape the whole list.
- **Keep at least one fast, cheap unit** for late-game objective grabs.
- **Do not over-invest in melee** unless you have a plan to close the distance safely.
- **Check what is actually in each box** — the range keeps expanding, so product contents change.

## Tactics That Win Games with the Halo Flashpoint Covenant

The single biggest mistake new players make is playing the Halo Flashpoint Covenant like a shooting army. You are not. You are an army of favorable trades.

- **Trade up, always.** Losing two Grunts to kill one elite model is a win.
- **Commit to one flank.** Spreading out plays into Spartan efficiency.
- **Use terrain aggressively.** Jackals in cover are a genuine problem; Jackals in the open are free points.
- **Accept that some models are already dead.** Position your screens so their deaths buy you tempo.
- **Play the scenario, not the kill count.** Most skirmish games are won on objectives.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Screen your Elites with Grunts | Charge Elites in alone |
| Concentrate fire on one target | Spread damage across the enemy |
| Hold objectives with cheap models | Hold objectives with your best unit |
| Use cover every single turn | Trade shots in the open |
| Plan two activations ahead | React one activation at a time |

Community reports also suggest that Covenant players who win consistently tend to be patient. The faction rewards players who let their opponent make the first mistake, then punish it with a concentrated activation spike.

## Covenant vs. Spartans: Reading the Matchup

| Factor | Advantage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Model count | Halo Flashpoint Covenant | More activations, more board presence |
| Per-model durability | Spartans | Every Spartan loss hurts them more |
| Melee | Halo Flashpoint Covenant | More dedicated close-combat options |
| Ranged accuracy | Spartans | Fewer, better shots |
| Objective play | Halo Flashpoint Covenant | Cheap bodies hold ground longer |
| Attrition math | Depends | Favor the side trading up |

The counter-intuitive truth of this matchup is that Spartans are often the ones under pressure. They cannot afford to lose models, so every exchange they take is a risk. Your job is to make them take exchanges they do not want — force them into cover they hate, onto objectives they cannot ignore, and into melee they cannot win.

## FAQ

### Is the Halo Flashpoint Covenant a good faction for beginners?

Yes, with one caveat. The faction is forgiving in list construction because cheap models absorb mistakes, but it punishes careless positioning. A balanced toolbox list is a good starting point; a pure swarm or pure elite list is better saved for later.

### How many models do I need for a Halo Flashpoint Covenant force?

Player experience suggests most Covenant lists run somewhere in the single digits to low teens of models, depending on the scenario size and how many cheap Grunts you bring. You generally need fewer models than you expect, but more variety.

### How do I deal with heavily armored opponents?

Concentrate your damage. A Halo Flashpoint Covenant force that spreads attacks across multiple targets rarely kills anything meaningful. Pick one target, commit multiple activations, and remove it from the board before moving on.

### Where do I find the rules and unit profiles?

The rules come with the core game, and additional unit profiles are included with each expansion box. Mantic's official site is the best place to confirm what is currently available before you buy.

The Covenant is not the easiest faction in Halo Flashpoint, but it is arguably the most interesting. Build a list with clear roles, play the scenario instead of the kill count, and trade up whenever you can — and you will find the Halo Flashpoint Covenant wins far more games than its fragile models suggest.
