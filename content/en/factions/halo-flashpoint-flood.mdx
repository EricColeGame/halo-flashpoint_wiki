## What the Halo Flashpoint Flood Faction Does Differently

If you have ever watched a Spartan fire team disappear under a wave of Infection Forms, you already understand why the Halo Flashpoint Flood faction is such a fascinating army to run. In Mantic Games' fast-playing skirmish game, the Flood is not a tidy, balanced gunline — it is a living tide that trades individual quality for numbers, board control, and relentless pressure. Learning the Halo Flashpoint Flood playstyle means accepting losses, thinking two activations ahead, and turning every destroyed model into a new problem for your opponent.

Most factions in Halo Flashpoint win by shooting better or maneuvering smarter. The Flood wins by refusing to run out of bodies. That single design idea shapes everything: how you deploy, which models you activate first, and how you convert a messy midfield into an unstoppable infection vector. You can see the wider game line and its faction releases on [Mantic Games' official Halo: Flashpoint hub](https://www.manticgames.com/), while the broader lore of the parasite lives at [Halo Waypoint](https://www.halowaypoint.com/).

### Faction Identity at a Glance

| Attribute | How the Halo Flashpoint Flood approaches it |
|---|---|
| Model count | High — you generally want more bodies than your opponent |
| Ranged power | Low to moderate; a few support forms carry the shooting |
| Melee threat | Excellent, especially once Combat Forms connect |
| Durability | Individually fragile, collectively stubborn |
| Speed | Fast Infection Forms create early pressure |
| Board control | Outstanding — you cover objectives by existing near them |
| Skill floor | Low to start, high to master |
| Best for | Players who enjoy attrition, tempo, and psychological pressure |

The key takeaway: the Flood is a tempo faction. You are not trying to out-shoot a Spartan kill team. You are trying to make every turn cost your opponent something, even when they win the exchange.

## Flood Unit Roles on the Tabletop

Halo Flashpoint Flood collections are built from a handful of recognizable organism types, each mapped to a clear battlefield job. Exact profiles, costs, and availability vary by release and scenario pack, so treat the table below as a role guide rather than a stat sheet — always confirm details in the rules that ship with your models.

| Unit archetype | Lore role | Tabletop job | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Infection Form | Tiny parasite that hijacks a host | Cheap swarm, objective grabber, tarpit | Fast, numerous, expendable | Almost no damage alone |
| Combat Form | Infected human or Elite | Frontline melee bruiser | Strong close-range threat, resilient | Predictable, wants cover to close |
| Carrier Form | Bloated spore carrier | Area denial, spawn support | Punishes clustering, feeds the swarm | Slow, obvious priority target |
| Pure Form (Stalker) | Specialized mutation | Flanker, gap-runner | Mobile, awkward to pin down | Needs support to finish fights |
| Pure Form (Ranged/Tank) | Heavy mutation | Fire support or anvil | Soaks attention, adds reach | Expensive, draws every shot |
| Proto-Gravemind | Emerging hive intelligence | Scenario objective or anchor | Thematic centerpiece, buffs the swarm | A loss condition you must protect |

A practical starting point is a broad core of Infection Forms, two or three Combat Forms as your damage dealers, and one heavier piece to hold the middle. Community reports suggest that new players lose more games by over-investing in big monsters than by running too many small ones.

### Reading the Swarm Like a Toolbox

- **Infection Forms** are your currency. Spend them to claim objectives, block charge lanes, and force wasted shots.
- **Combat Forms** are your scalpel. Send them only into fights they can win outright.
- **Carrier Forms** are your deterrent. Their presence changes where your opponent is willing to stand.
- **Pure Forms** are your answer to problems the swarm cannot solve by weight of numbers.

## Swarm Tactics: How to Run a Halo Flashpoint Flood Force

Because the Halo Flashpoint Flood faction rewards sequencing over raw stats, your activation order matters more than your list. The table below outlines a repeatable turn structure that works across most scenarios.

| Step | What you do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Deploy wide | Spread cheap models across multiple approach lanes | Denies your opponent a single kill zone |
| 2. Activate expendables first | Move Infection Forms early to claim ground | Forces reactions before your real threats commit |
| 3. Bait the big guns | Offer a tempting target to draw overwatch or heavy fire | Wastes enemy resources on low-value models |
| 4. Commit Combat Forms | Charge isolated or already-activated enemies | Guarantees favorable trades |
| 5. Hold a reserve | Keep one spawn-capable or fast unit back | Lets you respond to a collapsing flank |
| 6. Trade upward | Accept losses that cost you less than they cost them | Attrition math is how the Flood wins |

### Three Habits That Separate Good Flood Players

1. **Count activations, not wounds.** If you have more units left to activate than your opponent, you control the end of every round.
2. **Never charge into a fully intact enemy squad.** Flood models win by piling onto targets that are already committed, wounded, or out of position.
3. **Protect your anchor.** Whether it is a Proto-Gravemind, a spawn marker, or a scenario objective, one model usually holds your plan together. Guard it with bodies, not hope.

If your scenario includes spawning or reinforcement rules, treat them as your real win condition. Returning even a couple of cheap models to the table each round can swing a game that looks lost on the scoreboard.

## Matchups: Flood vs. UNSC, Covenant, and Mirror Lists

| Opponent | Their main strength | Your best answer | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| UNSC Spartans | Elite shooting and mobility | Swarm objectives, force split fire | Overwatch lanes and precision kills |
| UNSC Marines | Volume of fire | Close the distance fast, use cover | Massed ranged dice shredding your swarm |
| Covenant Elites | Melee parity plus ranged punch | Out-number them locally, trade cheap for expensive | Duelists that beat your Combat Forms one-on-one |
| Covenant Grunts/Jackals | Cheap bodies of their own | Win the attrition war with better trades | Shielded or defensive units stalling you out |
| Flood mirror match | Everything you do | Activate more efficiently, protect your anchor | A grind where tempo decides everything |

Against shooting-heavy factions, terrain is your best unit. Flood models that cross open ground in a straight line die in the open. Hug cover, use multiple small lanes, and make your opponent choose which threat to address. Against melee-capable factions like Elites, avoid fair fights entirely — gang up with two or three models, or refuse the charge and reposition.

Community reports consistently note that the Flood performs best in objective-based scenarios and worst in pure elimination games. If you can pick your mission, pick one that rewards board presence.

## Building and Painting Your Halo Flashpoint Flood Collection

A Halo Flashpoint Flood force is one of the cheaper factions to grow, because small models do the heavy lifting. Use this checklist when you plan a purchase or assemble a list.

| Slot | Recommended content | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Core swarm | Multiple Infection Form models | Buy more than you think you need |
| Damage dealers | Two to four Combat Forms | Magnetize or build for variety |
| Utility | One or two Carrier Forms | Great for area denial |
| Anchor | One heavy or leader organism | Your scenario centerpiece |
| Terrain | Ruins, corridors, scatter | Flood needs cover to close safely |
| Extras | Extra bases, tokens, dice | Keeps swarm turns fast |

On the hobby side, the Flood is a forgiving paint project. A pale, sickly basecoat, a wash in green or purple, and a light drybrush produces tabletop-ready results quickly. Batch-paint Infection Forms in groups of ten or more, then give Combat Forms a little extra attention so your opponent can instantly read which models matter. Consistent basing across the swarm also makes it easier to see unit boundaries during a crowded game.

Finally, label your units clearly. Flood armies put a lot of similar-looking models on the table, and ambiguity slows the game down. Tokens, colored base rims, or small markers solve this in seconds.

## FAQ

**Is the Flood a full faction in Halo Flashpoint, or just an expansion?**
The Flood is played as its own faction with dedicated models and rules, though the exact contents of each release have grown over time. Check the current product listings and the rules included with your models before building a list.

**What is the hardest part of playing the Halo Flashpoint Flood faction?**
Activation discipline. New players rush everything forward at once and lose their swarm piecemeal. Experienced players move cheap models first, hold threats back, and strike only when the math favors them.

**Can the Halo Flashpoint Flood faction compete against Spartans?**
Yes, but not by trading shots. Win through objectives, board control, and forcing your opponent to spend activations on low-value targets. In a straight firefight, Spartans usually come out ahead.

**How many models should a beginner Flood list include?**
Start with a broad core of cheap models plus a few specialists, then adjust after two or three games. Player experience suggests most new Flood lists fail because they are too elite, not too numerous.

**Do I need the core set to start the Flood?**
You will still need the core rules, dice, and measuring tools from Halo Flashpoint itself. The Flood models and their faction rules sit on top of that foundation.

The Flood rewards a very specific kind of player: someone who enjoys pressure, patience, and the slow realization on your opponent's face that the swarm is not getting smaller. Build wide, activate smart, and let the parasite do what it has always done — win by never truly dying.
