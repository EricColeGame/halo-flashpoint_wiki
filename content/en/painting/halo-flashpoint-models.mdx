## Why Halo Flashpoint Models Are Worth Your Paintbrush

Painting Halo Flashpoint models turns a box of grey plastic into a fireteam that looks like it walked off the screen. A good paint job is the difference between "toy soldiers" and "a diorama you can actually play a game on."

That distinction matters more here than in mass-battle games. The eye lingers on each figure, and small details like a gold visor or a heat-scorched barrel read clearly across the table.

## Preparing Halo Flashpoint Models Before the First Basecoat

Prep is unglamorous and absolutely non-negotiable. Paint adheres badly to mold release agent, and mold lines show through even a flawless layer job. Give yourself thirty to sixty minutes of prep per squad, and the painting stage becomes dramatically easier.

Here is a prep sequence that works for plastic and resin kits alike:

- **Remove mold lines** with the back edge of a hobby knife or a fine file. Check the helmet, shoulders, and weapon barrels — those are the spots people notice.
- **Wash the parts** in warm water with a drop of dish soap, then rinse and let them dry completely. This strips release agent and skin oils.
- **Dry-fit before gluing.** Sub-assemblies (arms, weapons, and backpacks held back on a paperclip) make it far easier to reach the chest and inner legs.
- **Fill gaps** with a little sprue goo or epoxy putty, then sand smooth once cured.
- **Prime in thin passes.** Two light coats beat one heavy coat every single time.

| Prep Step | Why It Matters | Tools | Time per Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mold line removal | Prevents visible ridges under paint | Hobby knife, files, sanding sticks | 3–5 min |
| Washing | Improves primer adhesion | Dish soap, warm water, soft brush | 1 min (batch) |
| Gap filling | Hides seams on armor joins | Sprue goo, putty, sculpting tool | 2–4 min |
| Sub-assembly | Reaches obscured areas | Clips, corks, super glue | 5 min |
| Priming | Gives paint a tooth to grip | Rattle can or airbrush | 10 min (batch) |

For primer color, think about your end result. Light grey or white makes olive drab and pale blues pop, while black primer suits dark Banished reds and creates instant shadow in recesses. A zenithal prime — dark base with a light spray from above — gives you a free map of highlights before you ever open a paint pot.

## Choosing a Paint Scheme for Your Halo Flashpoint Models

You have two paths: match the source material, or invent your own fireteam. Canonical schemes are satisfying and instantly readable. Custom schemes are more fun and let you build a narrative — a snow-camouflaged Spartan squad, a Banished cell that repainted its salvaged armor.

Either way, limit yourself to three to five colors per model plus metallics. Armor, accent, weapon, visor, and base. That restraint is what makes a squad look like a squad.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| UNSC Spartan (classic) | Olive drab green | Dark green wash | Yellow-green edge | Gold visor |
| UNSC Arctic | Light blue-grey | Blue-black wash | Near-white edge | Ice-blue visor |
| Covenant Elite | Deep blue or purple | Black-blue wash | Sky blue edge | Silver trim |

| Grunt | Rust orange | Brown wash | Bright orange edge | Grey methane rig |

Community reports consistently suggest that olive drab is the single hardest color to get right, because green paint covers unevenly over light primer. Two or three thin layers, not one thick one, is the fix.

## A Step-by-Step Painting Workflow for Halo Flashpoint Models

This is the sequence that produces reliable results. Work in batches of four to six figures so your drying time is never wasted.

### Step 1 — Basecoats and blocking in

Thin your paint to roughly the consistency of skim milk and apply two thin coats over the primer. Do not chase perfection yet; you are establishing flat color zones.

### Step 2 — Washes and panel lining

A wash does the heavy lifting on armor. Flow it into recesses and panel gaps rather than flooding the whole model, then tidy any pooling with a clean, damp brush. On Spartans, a targeted line of dark wash between armor plates reads as mechanical separation.

### Step 3 — Edge highlighting armor plates

Mjolnir armor is essentially a set of flat planes, which makes it ideal for edge highlighting. Drag the side of a loaded brush along the corner of each plate with a color one or two steps lighter than the base. Keep the line thin and consistent.

### Step 4 — Energy effects and details

Visors, plasma pistols, energy swords, and shield emitters all want the same treatment: paint white or off-white first, then layer a saturated color over it, then glaze a brighter tone in the center. The white underneath is what creates the illusion of light.

| Stage | Technique | Time per Model | What "Good" Looks Like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basecoat | Two thin layers | 10–15 min | Even coverage, no primer showing |
| Wash | Targeted recess shading | 3–5 min | Defined panels, no tide marks |
| Layer | Mid-tone on raised areas | 8–12 min | Smooth transitions |
| Edge highlight | Side-of-brush strokes | 10–15 min | Thin, unbroken lines |
| Details & glow | Layering, glazing | 10–20 min | Visor and plasma read as lit |
| Basing | Texture + drybrush | 10 min | Ties the model to the table |
| Varnish | Matte or satin spray | 2 min | Protected, non-glossy finish |

A rank-and-file trooper can reach a solid tabletop standard in about an hour once you are comfortable. Character models with capes, cloaks, or heavy glow effects typically run two to three hours. Player experience suggests batching is the biggest time saver — five models at once often take less than double the time of one.

## Basing and Finishing Your Halo Flashpoint Models

Basing is where a painted figure becomes a scene. It is also cheap and fast, which makes it the highest-value step in the entire hobby.

## Common Mistakes When Painting Halo Flashpoint Models

Even experienced painters fall into the same traps on armored sci-fi figures. Here is what to avoid and how to recover.

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | The Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy primer coat | Fills in visor slits and panel lines | Two light passes from 8–10 inches |
| One thick basecoat | Visible brushstrokes, lumpy armor | Thin paint, two or three layers |
| Flooding the whole model with wash | Muddy, greasy look | Targeted recess application only |
| Too many colors | Squad looks incoherent | Cap at five colors per model |
| Ignoring the visor | The focal point stays flat | White base, then saturated color |
| No varnish | Paint chips during play | Matte spray after basing |

A few extra habits separate clean paint jobs from great ones:

- **Use a wet palette.** Your paint stays workable for hours, and thinning becomes instinctive.
- **Paint a test model first.** Confirm your recipe on one figure before committing to eight.
- **Mount models on corks or handles.** Finger oils and hand cramps ruin detail work.
- **Photograph your work.** A phone photo exposes flaws your eye skips at arm's length.
- **Work in good light.** A daylight bulb reveals color accuracy that warm room lighting hides.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to paint a full squad of Halo Flashpoint models?**
A five-model squad at a clean tabletop standard typically takes four to six hours total, including prep and basing. Character models take longer because of glow effects and detail work, but the workflow above keeps the per-model time predictable.

**Should I assemble Halo Flashpoint models before painting?**
Mostly yes, with exceptions. Leave arms holding weapons, backpacks, and capes unglued so you can reach the chest and inner legs. Paint the parts on clips, then assemble and touch up the join lines.

**What paints do I need to start painting Halo Flashpoint models?**
A starter set with a primer, a handful of base colors, one or two washes, two metallics, and a matte varnish covers almost everything. Add a bright fluorescent tone for plasma and visor glow once you are comfortable with the basics.

**Can I use speed or contrast-style paints on armored models?**
Absolutely, and they are excellent for Grunts, fabric, and textured surfaces. On large flat armor plates they can pool unevenly, so many painters use them as a base layer and then add a traditional edge highlight to sharpen the result.
